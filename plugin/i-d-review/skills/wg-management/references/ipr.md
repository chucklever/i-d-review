# IPR and Copyright Obligations

The rules a chair most often gets wrong by overstating them.
Get the "whose duty" and "what is required" precise before
acting.

## The disclosure duty is personal

IPR disclosure under BCP 79 (RFC 8179, which obsoletes RFC 3979
and RFC 4879) is a personal obligation of the individual
contributor who is reasonably and personally aware of IPR that
covers or may cover a contribution (RFC 8179 §5.1.1).
Participants act as individuals. It is not the WG's duty, not
the chair's duty, and not automatically the employer's.

The chair cannot file another party's disclosure for them and
cannot waive the obligation. What the chair does is create the
conditions for compliance: ask.

## Timing

The standard is disclosure "as soon as reasonably possible
after the Contribution is submitted or made" (RFC 8179 §5.2.1).
BCP 79 does not set fixed "no-later-than" deadlines tied to
document stages — do not assert that it does. The
reasonably-possible standard is the rule; the later provisions
clarify late-awareness cases, not hard deadlines.

## When the chair should poll

BCP 79 imposes no duty on the chair to poll participants;
"poll" is not in it. Polling is best practice, recommended by
RFC 6702 (Informational), which suggests the chair remind and
confirm disclosure status at:

- draft presentation (RFC 6702 §3.1),
- WG adoption (§3.2),
- WG Last Call (§3.3), and
- IETF Last Call, via the AD (§3.5).

Appendix A of RFC 6702 gives sample reminder text. Get
affirmative statements; silence is not compliance. A disclosure
that lands mid-process can reset consensus and may warrant
re-running the adoption or Last Call poll. The shepherd
write-up later asks for per-author IPR confirmation (the
`wg-advancement` skill's `references/shepherd-writeup.md`).

## Copyright

Separately, contributors grant the rights the IETF Trust needs
to publish, under BCP 78 (RFC 5378, "Rights Contributors
Provide to the IETF Trust"). Confirm BCP 78 compliance for each
author as part of the publication request.
