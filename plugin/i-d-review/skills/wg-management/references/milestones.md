# Milestones

Milestones are part of the charter — the "Goals and
Milestones" listed among charter contents in RFC 2418 §2.2. A
milestone is a dated deliverable, for example "Aug 2026: submit
draft-ietf-example-widget to the IESG as Proposed Standard."

## Setting one

For a newly adopted work item, the chair proposes an
accompanying milestone. Because milestones are charter content,
they are coordinated with the responsible Area Director and
recorded in the datatracker. Adjusting a milestone date is
routine AD coordination; it is a change to the WG's *scope*,
not to a milestone date, that triggers the full rechartering
review (RFC 2418 §2.3, §5; see references/charter-and-scope.md).

## Judging realism

Base the date on evidence, not optimism:

- the WG's actual velocity on comparable past items;
- author availability and editor bandwidth;
- external dependencies — normative references to other WGs'
  work that must progress first;
- the expected number of revision and Last Call cycles.

A milestone that assumes no revision cycles, or that ignores a
blocking dependency, is not realistic. Prefer a date you would
defend to the AD.

## Overdue milestones

The datatracker flags overdue milestones, and they surface in
the AD's periodic WG review and at IESG telechats. Do not let
them sit red. Proactively re-date, drop, or re-scope the
milestone with the AD. Chronic slippage invites the AD to
question the work item's — or the WG's — viability, so treat
an overdue milestone as a signal to either recommit to a
credible date or formally abandon the deliverable.
