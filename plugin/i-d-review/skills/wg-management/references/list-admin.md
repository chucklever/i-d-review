# Mailing-List Moderation

The mailing list is the WG's primary decision-making venue
(RFC 2418 §2.2). Most moderation is ordinary chair facilitation
under the chair's discretion in conducting WG business (RFC
2418 §6.1): keeping threads on topic, summarizing, calling
questions, redirecting rehash. The escalation ladder below is
for the rare disruptive participant.

## Ordinary facilitation

- Keep discussion on the chartered work. Redirect off-topic or
  out-of-scope threads to the right venue.
- When a thread goes in circles, summarize the positions, state
  what would resolve it, and call the question. An unresolved
  loop is usually a missing decision, not a missing message.

## The escalation ladder for disruption

1. **Warn.** State on-list that the conduct is disruptive and
   what needs to change. Consult the responsible AD.
2. **Suspend posting (short).** A WG chair may suspend a
   disruptive individual's posting rights for up to 30 days
   without IESG approval, normally after explicit warning and
   consultation with the AD (RFC 3934, which is BCP 94 and
   updates the last paragraph of RFC 2418 §3.2). The person
   must still be able to *receive* list mail; only posting is
   suspended. The action is appealable under RFC 2026 §6.5.
3. **PR-action (long).** Removing an individual's posting
   rights for a longer period is a PR-action under RFC 3683
   (BCP 83). It is an IESG action, not a chair action: the AD
   introduces it, the IESG runs a last call on the general IETF
   list, and the IESG decides by consensus. A PR-action
   normally lasts at least a year, applies to posting only (not
   receiving), and is appealable.

Keep the two straight: the 30-day suspension is within the
chair's authority; anything longer requires the IESG.
