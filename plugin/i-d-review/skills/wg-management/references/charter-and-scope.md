# Charter Scope and Rechartering

The charter is the boundary of what a WG may work on. Its
contents — the administrative information, the "Description of
Working Group" (the scope), and the "Goals and Milestones" —
are set out in RFC 2418 §2.2. The scope text is the yardstick,
and the chair is the first-line judge of whether a proposed
work item fits.

## Judging scope

For a proposed draft, ask the in-scope questions from the
adoption criteria (RFC 7221 §2.2, criteria 2 and 8): is the
topic within the WG's chartered scope, and if not, is a simple
charter modification feasible and warranted? Assessing a draft
against those criteria is covered in the `internet-draft`
skill's `references/wg-adoption.md`; running the adoption call
once scope is settled is the `wg-advancement` skill's
`references/adoption-call.md`.

## Close to but not clearly in scope

This is a conversation with the responsible Area Director, not
a chair-only decision. In rough order of weight:

1. **Read the charter generously.** If the work plausibly falls
   under existing scope, confirm that reading with the AD and
   proceed.
2. **Recharter.** If the item needs the scope stretched, amend
   the charter. A charter change is negotiated with the AD,
   reviewed by the IAB, and approved by the IESG, and is posted
   to the IETF-Announce and new-work lists for community review
   (RFC 2418 §2.3; rechartering follows the same path, §5).
   This is the honest route for genuinely new scope.
3. **Decline as a WG item.** The work can still proceed as an
   individual or AD-sponsored submission.

Do not quietly adopt out-of-scope work. Rechartering exists
precisely to gate scope expansion, and the AD will bounce
out-of-scope work later.

## The charter constrains document change, too

The charter can also state the role of a document the WG starts
from — from a set of ideas to be used or ignored, up to a
deployed specification whose installed base must be protected.
That role sets how much the WG may change the document (RFC
7221 §4). When it bears on an adoption or scope decision,
surface the resulting change constraint as an explicit finding;
the `internet-draft` skill's `references/wg-adoption.md`
develops this.
